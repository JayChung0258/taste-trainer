---
title: Some users got the email twice
chapter: welcome-email
order: 3
mock: true
constraints:
  - Emails now go through an outbox table and a background job
  - The job retries when the provider times out
  - A few users report two identical welcome emails
options:
  - id: A
    label: Accept it; duplicates are rare and harmless
    gains:
      - No work at all
    costs:
      - Looks sloppy to users
      - The same bug will hurt later with emails that matter, like receipts
    fitsWhen: The email is purely friendly and duplicates are truly rare
    breaksWhen: You reuse the job for password resets or payment receipts
  - id: B
    label: Mark each outbox row as sent and check before sending
    gains:
      - Simple and easy to understand
    costs:
      - Still sends twice if the job crashes after sending but before marking
    fitsWhen: Rare duplicates are fine but common ones are not
    breaksWhen: Timeouts are frequent, so the crash window is hit often
  - id: C
    label: Send an idempotency key so the provider drops duplicates
    gains:
      - Duplicates are stopped at the source, even after crashes
    costs:
      - Depends on the provider supporting it
      - One more concept the team must understand
    fitsWhen: The provider supports idempotency keys and duplicates must not happen
    breaksWhen: You switch to a provider without that feature
itDependsOn: How bad a duplicate is, and how often the job fails halfway through.
flip:
  context: The email is a payment receipt
  notes:
    A: Not acceptable. Duplicate receipts confuse users and create support tickets.
    B: A reasonable first step, but the crash window now matters.
    C: Worth the extra concept. This is what idempotency keys are for.
sources:
  - title: Designing Data-Intensive Applications
    author: Martin Kleppmann
---

The outbox fixed lost emails. Now a different problem shows up. The job sends an email, the provider takes too long to answer, the job times out and retries, and the email goes out again.

```ts
for (const row of await outbox.pending()) {
  await emailService.send(row.email); // succeeded, but the response timed out
  await outbox.markSent(row.id);      // never reached
}
```

How do you deal with duplicates?
